Zaphod Beeblebrox: John’s Fastest Time of Day Model 3

John Chambers reserved a Model 3 Performance the day it was announced in the UK, took it to Thruxton two weeks after delivery, and turned it into a car that beat race and rally cars to Fastest Time of Day at the Watergate Bay Sprint.

Who are you?

I’m John Chambers, and I’m based in the South West of England.

What do you drive?

A 2019 Model 3 Performance and a 2020 Model 3 Long Range, both in Midnight Silver Metallic with black interiors.

Was this your first EV? What made you choose a Tesla?

The Model 3 Performance was my first EV. Its performance was probably the main reason I wanted one, but I was also curious about how practical and enjoyable it would be to own compared with all the petrol cars I’ve had over the years.

What was buying it like?

I reserved my Model 3 Performance in March 2016, the day it was announced in the UK. It was a long wait before I could see or drive one, and I did have some doubts about whether I’d still want it when the time came to pay.

Luckily, I had a trip to Los Angeles in 2018 and managed to rent one while I was there. After driving it out of the car park and 100 yards down the street, I knew there was no way I’d be cancelling my reservation!

Delivery day came 19 long months later. Back then, handover still included a little presentation and someone to show you around the car. I’d already watched every YouTube video on the Model 3 and had been on various Tesla forums and groups for three years, so I knew how it all worked. I was out of the showroom and on the road with no time to lose!

We had two performance BMWs at the time, and the plan was to keep the better, faster one. But it felt old, clunky and slow after driving the Model 3 Performance, so a year later we replaced it with a new Model 3 Long Range. We haven’t missed spending £100 a time at smelly petrol stations one bit.

Why did you start modifying?

I could tell the Model 3 would make a very competitive car for track days and even competition (I’ve been involved in motorsport since the 1980s), but I knew it would need some changes to work properly on track. That’s no different to most petrol road cars, even “high performance” ones, and a 550bhp, 1,850kg four-door EV was going to need a few tweaks.

So I booked a Thruxton track day just two weeks after picking the car up, and gave it a good thrashing to see what needed doing. Predictably, brakes, suspension and tyres were top of the list. I’d already done quite a bit of research, as a few owners in the US were competing in autocross and driving on track.

What was your first mod?

Uprated brake pads and fluid, and a set of Michelin Cup 2 tyres. At the same time, I had the car partially covered in PPF to protect the paint, and had the chrome trim, door handles and badges blacked out. Nothing too extreme, as this car is all about go, not show!

Talk us through your mods so far.

It’s quite a long list for the Performance…

Suspension
  • Öhlins TTX two-way adjustable dampers with Eibach race springs (Tevo/Öhlins)
  • Floating top damper mounts, adjustable front upper control arms, and adjustable rear toe and camber arms (Redwood)
  • Adjustable uprated front and rear anti-roll bars (Eibach), with adjustable uprated drop links (Redwood)
  • Uprated front compression rod and lower control arm bearings, rear traction arms and rear trailing arms (Mountain Pass Performance)
  • Lowered by about 35mm, with the geometry set up for track tyres and motorsport (more camber and so on)
Brakes: Tevo big brake kit
  • AP Racing CP-9660 six-pot front calipers, with 378 x 34mm vented front discs and 354 x 21mm vented rear discs (Tevo/AP Racing)
  • Pagid RSL29 front pads and Carbotech XP10 rear pads
  • A Tevo master cylinder brace
  • HEL braided brake lines and Castrol React SRF brake fluid
Wheels, tyres and the rest
  • Tevo T4 Forged 20 x 9″ wheels in satin black
  • Michelin Cup 2, Cup 2 Connect and Cup 2 R, Nankang AR-1 and Bridgestone Potenza Sport tyres
  • Carbon fibre front splitter and rear spoiler (Maier EV)
  • Alcantara interior trim (DAJ Auto) and steering wheel (Royal Steering Wheels)

Our Long Range is mainly a road car, so it’s not as extreme, but it has a few important mods:

  • A full semi-active suspension system (Tevo/TracTive)
  • Uprated front lower control arms (Powerflex)
  • Uprated brake fluid and front pads (Castrol/Brembo/Pagid)
  • A Tevo master cylinder brace
  • Furry dice (not really!)

What’s your favourite mod, and why?

The Öhlins two-way dampers on the Performance. They transformed the original Tesla suspension, which wasn’t the best. Tesla has improved it with the adaptive system on the newer Model 3 Highland, but it’s very difficult to beat a really good conventional adjustable coilover setup from one of the leading motorsport names.

Which mod gave you the best value for money?

The Tevo master cylinder brace. It makes a significant difference to brake feel and confidence when you’re braking hard from high speed, and considering how it’s made and what it costs, it’s a bargain. It’s a work of art!

Is there anything you’d do differently?

There’s nothing I’d skip if it makes the car faster. Everything on both cars was chosen for being the very best available and able to cope with being driven hard, on the road and in competition.

But mods are evolving all the time, so I’d still look at replacing some if better options come along.

Any suppliers or fitters you’d recommend?

  • Parts: Tevo Solutions, Mountain Pass Performance and Eibach
  • Paint correction, protection, detailing and wraps: Auto-Protek and Gemclean
  • Steering wheel repairs and replacements: Royal Steering Wheels

How do you use the car?

The Performance is mainly used on track now, but it still gets driven on the road from time to time. The Long Range is our daily driver.

Has the car taken you anywhere memorable?

The most memorable trip in recent years was the Watergate Bay Sprint in 2023. It’s a two-day closed-road competition along the Cornish coast, where all kinds of cars race against the clock to set the fastest time.

A few of us turned up in EVs that year, and I managed to set Fastest Time of Day on the Sunday, beating all the petrol cars, including some potent, highly modified race and rally cars.

What reactions do you get from other people?

On track and in competition, once people see how fast these cars are, it seems to be a mix of curiosity and contempt, depending on how open-minded they are.

On the road, there are enough Teslas around now that they don’t get much of a reaction any more, although there was a period when I’d come across the odd “EV hater” trying to make things difficult.

Otherwise, people at events, garages and workshops are usually interested in hearing what I’ve done to the cars and how they perform in competition. Most genuine motorsport people don’t care where the power comes from. They just want more of it, so they can set a faster time!

How did you find MT3UK?

Through Facebook.

What’s your favourite MT3UK moment so far?

It was good to meet up at the Castle Combe EV day recently and put faces to names.

What advice would you give someone about to modify their Tesla?

Do your research carefully before wasting time and money on mods that won’t meet your expectations and that you’ll probably end up replacing. There’s a lot of very misleading marketing out there, and nasty, cheap parts dressed up to look good.

The MT3UK online community is a great place for unbiased help and advice, so go there first!

What’s next for the car?

Nothing more for the moment, as it’s probably still got the best bits available on it.
