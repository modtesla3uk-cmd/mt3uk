Founding the MT3UK Community

The first in our owner interview series. Richard talks about buying his first EV, why he started MT3UK, and how a white Model 3 became one of the most recognisable cars in the group.

Who are you?

I’m Richard, and I started the MT3UK Modified Tesla Community Facebook page.

What was it like to buy an EV, and was this your first?

This was my first EV, although I’d had a hybrid before. A conversation at work caught my interest: a car that’s quick, costs little to run and doesn’t look half bad. Buying a Tesla isn’t like buying from a traditional garage, where you sit down with someone to go through finance you can’t afford. Tesla is a tech company and doesn’t sell cars the conventional way.

I remember walking into the Tesla store in Milton Keynes after spending hours obsessing over the specs on the website. The test drive was just 20 minutes in a Model 3 SR (Standard Range Rear-Wheel Drive). I was handed the keys and left to drive. Even speed-limited, it was enough to realise that the acceleration of the base model was simply mind-blowing. I dropped it back off and was left to ponder.

I ordered the car online, just picking my preferences and paying the £200 deposit. I think I changed my order several times, but I finally settled on a 2019 Model 3 Performance in white. I was locked in, and the waiting began.

Like many waiting for those first Model 3s to arrive in 2019, I felt like a child waiting for Christmas. I checked the Facebook owners’ groups multiple times a day for news, as literally hundreds of us had ordered as soon as the books opened. Finally, September came, and I got the email from Tesla Sales! I booked a train to Heathrow and collected my brand-new Tesla Model 3 from West Drayton on 17 September 2019.

What does MT3UK stand for?

Modified Tesla 3 UK. Some might ask why I didn’t use TM3UK for Tesla Model 3 UK. I wanted to differentiate us from other groups using the conventional acronym and create a community specifically focused on modifying Teslas.

Aren’t there already a lot of groups for Tesla owners?

Yes, there are lots of Tesla groups on Facebook, some dating back before the Model 3 really took off in the UK. A few groups appeared for new owners, and it was exciting to connect with others waiting to hear when their cars would arrive.

You have to remember this was a very new car. Not only was it an EV, but it appealed to a much broader audience. The Model S was available, but a large saloon had limited appeal at the time. The Model 3 promised to be an everyday car with plenty of space for a family, perfectly suited to our narrow UK roads.

Why modify a Tesla? Aren’t they all just “microwaves on wheels”?

Who doesn’t like a microwave? Some people feel their cars are an extension of themselves. The factory build is designed for mass appeal, and as Teslas grew in popularity, we started seeing more and more of them on the road. For me, modifying was about trying to differentiate my car from all the others.

Tell me about your modifying journey.

For the first 3 years I was as happyish. it's more than enough on the road, the kids loved playing Beach Buggy and driving the car like daddy does, Oh and leaving nice fingerprints all over it!

The modifying started with the ride height. The Performance model sits slightly lower, but it still has a good amount of wheel arch gap. Aside from aesthetics, the factory suspension isn’t amazing at motorway speeds. There are now so many options for lowering. Instead of listing them all, I’ll just say that whatever you can think of, there is an aftermarket option available. I eventually moved to adjustable coilovers as it later became dual purpose.

The original front bumper has a somewhat frog-like look, which isn’t the car’s best feature. Addressing the front end was high on my list from an aesthetics perspective, leading to exterior upgrades like the Robot Craftsman Crypton front bumper.

How has the car evolved since then?

In 2022, just over three years in, I was considering changing the car. Then I went on track with Tevo Solutions at Llandow Circuit, and it sparked something in me. I started taking the car on track and changed a few things, mostly around the brakes and suspension. I’ll go into the track-specific upgrades another time.

The Wrap

Roll on to 2024, and I felt the car either needed to go or needed some significant changes. So I decided to look into getting it wrapped instead of selling it. I’d never had a car wrapped before and had no idea what was possible. All I knew was that I wanted it to stand out without being too shouty, and I finally settled on a colour-shifting wrap, specifically Avery Dennison Gloss Hidden Forest. It is probably the number one thing that sets my car apart from all the other Model 3s out there today.
