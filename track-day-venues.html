Track Day Venues: What to Expect

The circuits members turn up to most, and the ones you'll see cars from in the Track Days gallery. Each one loads your Tesla differently, so it's worth knowing what you're driving into before you book.

Snetterton

LOCATIONNorfolk
LAYOUTSnetterton 300, around 2.97 miles
CHARACTERFormer WWII airfield, fast and flowing

Snetterton's 300 layout is one of the longest in the UK and built around the Bentley Straight, a long full-throttle run that ends in a hard braking zone into the Bomb Hole. It's a brake and cooling test as much as a handling one: expect sustained high speed followed by repeated heavy stops, which is exactly where stock pads, fluid and thermal management start to show their limits.

Thruxton

LOCATIONHampshire
LAYOUTAround 2.4 miles
CHARACTERWidely rated the fastest permanent circuit in the UK

Another ex-airfield circuit, Thruxton is defined by long, sweeping, high-speed corners like Campbell and Club rather than hard braking zones. Average speeds are high and sustained, so this one leans hard on suspension geometry and tyre grip under prolonged lateral load. It's a good gauge of how your alignment and tyre choice hold up once cornering forces stay high for several seconds at a time, not just a single apex.

Cadwell Park

LOCATIONLincolnshire
LAYOUTAround 2.2 miles
CHARACTERNicknamed the "Mini Nürburgring"

Cadwell Park is the opposite of the other two: tight, twisty and hilly, with blind crests and elevation changes through sections like the Mountain. It rewards precise, technical driving over outright speed, and punishes soft suspension and worn brakes on the tighter hairpins. If Snetterton and Thruxton test sustained speed, Cadwell tests how your car settles and recovers between quick direction changes.

Castle Combe

LOCATIONWiltshire
LAYOUTAround 1.85 miles
CHARACTERAnother ex-airfield circuit, flat and deceptively fast

Castle Combe is short, but its flat, wide-open layout keeps average speeds high with very little let-up, from the fast Avon Rise into Quarry Corner. There's less hard braking than Snetterton and fewer direction changes than Cadwell, so the biggest load here is sustained cornering grip and tyre temperature over a short, quick lap rather than one single punishing zone. It's also notoriously bumpy in places, which is a good stress test for suspension bushings and anything already on its way out.
